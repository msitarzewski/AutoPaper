Settings

On a Mac, Settings… in the menu (⌘ ,), with panes General, Providers, Accounts, Memory, Budget and About. On Windows, Settings in the navigation pane, with the same pages (Keys for Accounts). On Linux, Preferences (Ctrl ,), with General, Providers, Keys, Memory and Budget; About is in the main menu. Each reopens where you left it.

Accounts (Keys)

OpenAI API key, Google Gemini API key, and Key for the OpenAI-compatible server’s address (only if it asks for one). Saved as you type, half a second after you stop on a Mac, to the Keychain (service com.autopaper.credentials), Credential Manager (AutoPaper:…, kept on this PC only) or the Secret Service. An empty field removes the key. Each has a Get a key link.

About

Moods and keywords

Limits and defaults for moods and keywords
WhatLimit or default
Mood name1 to 40 characters, unique (ignoring case). New moods are “New mood”; a duplicate is “name copy”.
Number of moodsNo limit; at least one, so the last can’t be deleted.
Keywords per moodUp to 64, each up to 40 characters, unique within the mood (ignoring case). New ones are Must.
Surprise0 to 100; new moods start at 35. Faithful below 25, Fresh below 50, Adventurous below 75, Wild from 75.
Shared by every moodThe schedule, providers, budget, quiet period, echoes setting, memory and taste.

The first mood in an older library is named from its first two keywords that aren’t Avoids (“Rain, Beach”), otherwise “My mood”. Switching moods makes nothing by itself. Echoes are three times as likely to come from the current mood’s wallpapers.

Writing a scene

  1. The writer is sent the current mood’s keywords and Surprise, up to 20 recent wallpapers’ descriptions (“don’t repeat these”), up to 6 liked and 6 disliked things, and your language. It’s asked for four ideas in one answer, each with a title (at most 60 characters), a one-line description, the place, what’s in it, the time of day, weather, season, mood, palette, style, composition, and the prompt (at most 2,500 characters).
  2. The temperature is 0.4 + 0.8 × Surprise, where the model accepts one. Ideas of its own: none when Faithful, up to 1 when Fresh, 2 when Adventurous, 3 when Wild.
  3. Each idea is checked: every Must in the prompt, no Avoid anywhere in the prompt, title or description, nothing empty, nothing too long.
  4. Each valid idea is scored: novelty × (1 − 0.3 × Surprise) + taste × (1 − 0.6 × Surprise) + a random amount up to 0.3 × Surprise. The best one that isn’t too close to anything you’ve had wins.
  5. If none is new enough, the writer is asked again, at most twice, naming up to 8 past wallpapers it came too close to; then the least similar valid idea is taken. If asking again doesn’t help, it stops early. When 3 of a mood’s last 5 wallpapers had to be taken that way, AutoPaper says your keywords are narrow.
  6. If the writer or the painter refuses an idea, it’s asked once for a gentler, plainly described scene; a second refusal is reported.

Titles and descriptions are written in your language; everything else, and always the prompt, in English. Prompts ask for a landscape picture without text, letters, logos, watermarks or interface, with calm space where desktop icons sit, and never mention an Avoid.

Memory

  • Each wallpaper is remembered as an embedding of its title, description, place, contents, time, weather, season, mood and palette, made on your computer by bge-small-en-v1.5 (BAAI, MIT licence, 384 dimensions), which is part of the app.
  • A new idea is compared with every past wallpaper: similarity × a weight for its age, which is 1 within the quiet period and then halves every half quiet period. At 0.79 or more, it’s too close.
  • Memory is kept when images are cleared to save space, and when history is cleared with Keep memory.
  • Without the model, a simpler word-based comparison is used (threshold 0.70), which catches only near-identical ideas, and Settings → Memory says Reduced.
  • Each image also gets a 64-bit picture fingerprint; a near-copy of a recent image is noted in the log, not painted again (that would be billed twice).

Echoes

  • Which wallpapers can echo: ones older than the quiet period, not disliked, not cleared from history, and with no echo in their line within the quiet period. Liked ones are three times as likely; older ones more likely (twice as likely at a year old, up to four times at three years); ones from the current mood three times as likely.
  • How often: the Echoes setting’s chance, rolled for each scheduled wallpaper when one can echo. Make an Echo in History works on any wallpaper, whenever you like.
  • What changes: 2 things below Surprise 50, 3 from 50: weather, time of day, season, the passage of time (decay or renewal), viewpoint or distance, era or medium. Your Avoids still apply; the mood’s Musts and Maybes don’t.
  • What counts as an echo: similar to its original (0.72 or more) but not a copy (below 0.93), with at least one of the asked-for changes visible, and new against everything else. Each is linked to its original, with a note saying what changed. A scheduled echo that doesn’t work out becomes an ordinary new wallpaper.

Taste

  • Like or Dislike adds one to a tally for each thing the wallpaper was made of: its place, contents, palette, mood, style, time of day, weather, season and the keywords used. Changing your mind undoes the old rating; choosing the same again clears it.
  • Tallies halve every year.
  • Something becomes a hint to the writer (“tends to like”, “tends to dislike”) once about three ratings agree: at least 65% liked, or at most 35%. Up to 6 of each are sent.
  • Taste is a hint, never a rule, and counts for less the higher Surprise is. Avoid is the rule.

Painting and sizes

You don’t choose a size: AutoPaper asks for the one closest to your main display’s shape, as large as the painter allows and no larger than your display. Until the app has told it the display’s size, it aims at 3840 × 2160.

The pixel cap for Z-Image Turbo and Krea 2 Turbo is where their image decoder fails in ComfyUI on Apple silicon; it applies on every computer, since AutoPaper can’t tell what a ComfyUI server runs on. The bundled workflows take 8 steps (Z-Image Turbo, Krea 2 Turbo) and 25 (Qwen-Image 2.1).

Every image is checked before use: PNG, JPEG or WebP (judged from the file itself), at most 50 MB, 64 megapixels and 16,384 px a side, turned upright if its camera data says so, and flattened if it has transparency.

Putting it on screen

  • Every display gets the same wallpaper, made for it: cropped from the centre to the display’s shape, scaled to its exact pixels (Lanczos), lightly sharpened only when enlarged more than 1.25 times, and saved as a JPEG at quality 90 without metadata. On Linux, the Wallpaper portal takes one picture for every monitor, made for the largest.
  • macOS, Over my wallpaper (the default): one borderless, click-through window per display, just above the desktop picture and below the icons, on every Space. Wallpapers cross-fade in 1.6 seconds (0.3 with Reduce Motion). Your desktop picture is never changed; quitting closes the windows.
  • macOS, As my wallpaper: sets each display’s desktop picture, after noting your own for each, so it shows in Mission Control and on the lock screen. macOS doesn’t let apps put back its moving wallpapers exactly.
  • Windows: sets each monitor’s wallpaper, positioned to fill, and the lock screen. Before the first, it keeps a copy of each monitor’s picture, its position and colour, and whether it was a slideshow or Windows Spotlight, and puts them back when AutoPaper quits, is paused, or you choose Restore my wallpaper.
  • Linux: through the desktop’s Wallpaper portal, which may ask once. On GNOME (outside Flatpak), your own wallpaper settings are recorded first and put back when AutoPaper quits, is paused, or you choose Restore my wallpaper.

The schedule, failures and the budget

  • The schedule counts from the last new wallpaper, of any kind: on the schedule, New Wallpaper Now, a disliked one’s replacement, or an echo. Bringing back an old one doesn’t count. A scheduled wallpaper starts early by its estimated time (at most half an interval), so it’s ready when due.
  • The apps check when it’s due, and again when the computer wakes or is unlocked.
  • After a failure, the next try waits 10 minutes, then 20, 40, and 60, or longer if the provider asks (at most an hour). The first failure in a row that isn’t yours to fix brings back a wallpaper you liked, unless you chose Keep the current one. A painting ComfyUI couldn’t do waits for the next scheduled time instead. Cancelling isn’t a failure.
  • Before each paid request, its estimated cost is checked against what’s left of the month’s budget. Each paid request counts as soon as it’s answered, even if the wallpaper doesn’t work out. Local providers, OpenAI-compatible servers and Demo cost nothing.
  • Estimated prices (as of October 5, 2026): OpenAI’s default painter at 3840 × 2160 about $0.026 (Standard) or $0.10 (High); Nano Banana 2 about $0.10 at 2K and $0.15 at 4K. Writing a scene costs about a tenth of a cent with OpenAI’s default writer, and about half a cent with Gemini’s.

Time estimates

  • Every finished request to a provider is recorded on your computer: writing or painting, provider, server address (for your own servers), model, size, steps, and how many seconds it took. The newest 50 are kept for each provider and model. They’re never sent.
  • An estimate is the median of comparable records, each weighted by age (a 30-day-old one counts half) and scaled to the size and steps wanted. With no records, there’s no estimate.
  • While painting with ComfyUI, progress follows its steps, at the pace measured in that painting; otherwise it follows the estimate, showing at most 95%.
  • ComfyUI jobs have no fixed time limit. One is stopped after 3 minutes without progress following a step (or 4 times its slowest step), 10 minutes in a step-less part, or past the longer of 30 minutes and 3 times its estimate. Hosted writers have 60 seconds and painters 240; Ollama and OpenAI-compatible writers 180 seconds, painters 300. The Network page has every limit.

Accessibility

AutoPaper aims for WCAG 2.2 AA as W3C’s WCAG2ICT applies it to desktop software, and for Apple’s, Microsoft’s and GNOME’s accessibility guidelines. This site aims for WCAG 2.2 AA directly.

  • Spoken names: every control and image has one. Wallpapers are described by their one-line description and, for an echo, what changed; keywords say their weight (“rain, Must”); Surprise its value and band; moods whether they’re current (“Rainy beach, current mood”); Like and Dislike whether they’re on; progress its percentage and time left.
  • Announcements: stages, problems and changes (a mood made current, a keyword moved) are announced politely.
  • Keyboard: designed to work entirely from the keyboard, including reordering and every History action. See Keyboard shortcuts.
  • Colour: never the only sign. The current mood has a checkmark and the word; ratings and echoes have badges with words.
  • Motion: wallpapers cross-fade. While one is painting, the picture in the window ripples (macOS) or a veil breathes (Windows) or fades in once (Linux); with reduced motion on, it holds still. New wallpapers can be paused.
  • Contrast and transparency: standard controls throughout, so Increase Contrast, high-contrast themes and Reduce Transparency apply.

Each app has been checked through its system’s accessibility interface (the macOS accessibility API, UI Automation on Windows, AT-SPI on Linux) in development builds. Full passes with VoiceOver, Narrator and Orca are still to come; if something is hard to use, please open an issue.

Five App Intents on macOS, available to Siri (with ready-made phrases) and the Shortcuts app. Windows and Linux have none yet.

For developers

Every decision lives in one Rust library, autopaper-core: composing, memory, echoes, taste, moods, the schedule, the budget, providers, the network rules, images and storage. The three apps are native (SwiftUI on macOS, WinUI 3 on Windows, GTK 4 and libadwaita on Linux) and use it through UniFFI, or directly on Linux. Each provider is one file in core/src/providers/. The core’s tests run with cargo test. See CONTRIBUTING.md.

The repository includes autopaper, a command-line tool over the same core, with keys from a .env file:

autopaper moods                      # the moods, the current one marked, with their keywords
autopaper compose                    # one writing request: the ideas, their checks and scores
autopaper generate                   # make a wallpaper now, with the progress an app shows
autopaper models images              # what a Model menu lists for the painter
autopaper --display 3840x2160 run    # what an app's timer does: make one if it's due
autopaper simulate --days 1100       # years of wallpapers with stand-in providers: memory, echoes, taste

The source is on GitHub; its README has the steps to build each app.
